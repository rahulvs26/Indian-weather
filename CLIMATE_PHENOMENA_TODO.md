## 1 · Monsoon system (M)

- [ ] **M1 · Southwest Monsoon onset over Kerala** — the seasonal wind reversal that opens India's principal rainy season. Criteria: 60% of 14 designated stations report ≥2.5 mm rain for two consecutive days, westerlies hold to 600 hPa, INSAT OLR <200 W m⁻². **Season:** normal onset 1 June; Jun–Sep. **Regions:** Kerala first, then country-wide. **Impact:** sets the country's main rainy season.
  - Source: [mausam.imd.gov.in · onset](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/contents/onset.php)

- [ ] **M2 · Northern Limit of Monsoon (progressive advance)** — monsoon advances northward in surges; NLM = northernmost limit reached on any day. **Season:** Jun–Jul; entire country normally covered around **8 July** (2020 revised normals; legacy normal was 15 July).
  - Sources: [IMD · Monsoon FAQ](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/monsoonfaq.pdf) · [MAUSAM journal](https://mausamjournal.imd.gov.in/index.php/MAUSAM/article/view/33)

- [ ] **M3 · Withdrawal of SW Monsoon & retreating monsoon** — no withdrawal before 1 Sept; criteria: 5-day rainfall cessation, 850-hPa anticyclone, moisture reduction. Country-wide withdrawal only after 1 Oct, completed ~15 Oct; NW-India withdrawal delayed >2 weeks vs the legacy 1 Sept normal. **Season:** Oct–Dec is IMD's post-monsoon (retreating) season.
  - Source: [mausam.imd.gov.in · withdrawal](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/contents/withdrawal.php)

- [ ] **M4 · Active & break monsoon spells (intraseasonal variability)** — active/vigorous: rainfall 1.5–4× / >4× normal; weak/subdued: <½ normal. Break monsoon: trough shifts to the Himalayan foothills (or vanishes) with low-level easterlies absent >2 days → foothill/Brahmaputra floods, dry central India. Driven by the 40-day mode, 14–15-day quasi-biweekly oscillation, and the MJO.
  - Source: [mausam.imd.gov.in · monsoon activity](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/contents/monsoon_activity.php)

- [ ] **M5 · Monsoon trough** — semi-permanent elongated low-pressure area from the heat low (NW Rajasthan–Pakistan) to the Head Bay of Bengal; normal axis Ganganagar–Allahabad–Kolkata–Head Bay; oscillates ~5° daily. South of normal = active monsoon; north/foothills = break. **Season:** Jun–Sep — India's "equatorial trough of northern summer".
  - Sources: [IMD Pune · glossary](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/Reports/glossary.pdf) · [IMD Pune · Synoptic Meteorology training notes](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/training/training%20notes/Synoptic%20Meteorology-IMTC.pdf)

- [ ] **M6 · Monsoon lows & depressions** — the principal rain-bearing systems; lows intensify into depressions (17–33 kt); average **2 per monsoon month**. Genesis north of 18°N in the NW Bay in Jul–Aug, shifting to the central Bay in Sept; drift WNW with heaviest rain in the SW quadrant; rarely become cyclones (high shear).
  - Source: [IMD · Monsoon FAQ](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/monsoonfaq.pdf)

- [ ] **M7 · Northeast (winter) monsoon** — Oct–Dec reversal to northeasterlies; main rainy season of the south peninsula, ~60% of annual rainfall in coastal Tamil Nadu districts. Declared when SW-monsoon withdrawal reaches 15°N with persisting easterlies over Tamil Nadu to 850 hPa (trough ~13°N near Chennai). 2025 commencement announced 16 Oct over TN–Puducherry–Karaikal, Coastal AP, Rayalaseema, S. Interior Karnataka, Kerala.
  - Source: [IMD press release, 16 Oct 2025](https://internal.imd.gov.in/press_release/20251016_pr_4394.pdf)

- [ ] **M8 · Sub-tropical westerly jet (winter)** — steady westerly jet at ~27°N, ~200 hPa / ~12 km across India. Weakens and shifts north of the Himalayas at monsoon onset (permitting the SW monsoon); dips south in winter/break phases with western disturbances.
  - Source: [IMD Pune · Synoptic Meteorology training notes](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/training/training%20notes/Synoptic%20Meteorology-IMTC.pdf)

- [ ] **M9 · Tropical easterly jet (summer)** — upper-level easterly jet at ~150 hPa in July, centred near Chennai's latitude, running Vietnam → West Africa as Tibetan High outflow. Strong/south of normal in active spells; weak/ shifted north (~20°N) in breaks; weakens in September.
  - Source: [IMD · Monsoon FAQ](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/monsoonfaq.pdf)

- [ ] **M10 · ITCZ shift over India** — the equatorial trough/ITCZ migrates from ~5°S (January) to 12–15°N (July); its northward movement and merging with the heat trough — via three-stage Andaman Sea, Arabian Sea and North Bay systems — governs onset and advance.
  - Source: [IMD Pune · Synoptic Meteorology training notes](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/training/training%20notes/Synoptic%20Meteorology-IMTC.pdf)

- [ ] **M11 · Tibetan Plateau heating (Tibetan High)** — intense heating of the elevated plateau builds a warm upper anticyclone (max intensity 200 hPa); its outflow feeds the easterly jet and its position steers monsoon lows and rainfall. **Season:** Jun–Sep.
  - Source: [IMD · Monsoon FAQ](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/monsoonfaq.pdf)

- [ ] **M12 · Land–sea thermal contrast & heat low (incl. seasonal migration)** — differential land–ocean heating drives the seasonal wind reversal; the heat low over NW Rajasthan–Pakistan (shallow, to 850 hPa) acts as a "suction device" pulling moist air along the monsoon trough; an intense heat low favours a good monsoon. The heat trough migrates with the season: Mar — peninsula → Apr — Vidarbha/MP → May — Punjab → Jun–Sep — NW Rajasthan & adjoining Pakistan (one of IMD's semi-permanent monsoon systems).
  - Sources: [IMD Pune · glossary](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/Reports/glossary.pdf) · [IMD Pune · Synoptic Meteorology training notes](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/training/training%20notes/Synoptic%20Meteorology-IMTC.pdf)

- [ ] **M13 · Western Ghats orography, off-shore trough/vortex & rain shadow** — monsoon winds striking the Ghats form an off-shore vortex; the off-shore trough (Gujarat–Kerala, to ~0.9 km) gives heavy west-coast rain (Mahabaleshwar max; Mumbai less than the ghat stations); the lee side is the rain shadow (Pune much drier).
  - Source: [IMD · Monsoon FAQ](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/monsoonfaq.pdf)

- [ ] **M14 · Mawsynram & Cherrapunji (Khasi Hills) extreme rainfall** — the Khasi Hills of Meghalaya funnel the monsoon; Cherrapunji and Mawsynram record world-class rainfall totals, studied in IMD's MAUSAM journal. **Season:** Jun–Sep.
  - Sources: [IMD Pune · Synoptic Meteorology training notes](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/training/training%20notes/Synoptic%20Meteorology-IMTC.pdf) · [MAUSAM journal](https://mausamjournal.imd.gov.in/index.php/MAUSAM/article/view/3486)

## 2 · Ocean–atmosphere oscillations & teleconnections (O)

- [ ] **O1 · El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO)** — coupled Pacific ocean–atmosphere phenomenon; El Niño = warm central/east equatorial Pacific, La Niña = cool; events recur every 2–7 yrs, develop in spring, peak Nov–Feb. El Niño generally suppresses the Indian Summer Monsoon (below-normal rain, dry spells, heat waves; drought years 1982, 1987, 2002, 2009, 2015); La Niña strengthens it (above-normal rain, flood potential) — nationwide, mainly central/NW India. Relationship is inverse but **not deterministic** (IMD Pune: ~42% of El Niño years had below-normal ISMR).
  - Sources: [IMD · ENSO & IOD FAQ](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/FAQ_ENSO_IOD.pdf) · [IMD Pune · FAQ](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/faq.php)

- [ ] **O2 · ENSO Modoki (central-Pacific El Niño)** — warm SST anomalies in the central Pacific flanked by cold anomalies east and west (tripole; monitored by the ENSO Modoki Index, Ashok et al. 2007); distinct teleconnections from canonical ENSO and shown to influence Indian rainfall. Interplay with IOD/MJO makes North Indian Ocean cyclogenesis complex (more frequent cyclones in some Modoki–IOD combinations).
  - Sources: [JAMSTEC · El Niño Modoki](https://www.jamstec.go.jp/aplinfo/sintexf/e/elnmodoki/about_elnm.html) · [Natural Hazards study](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/nathaz/v68y2013i2p1057-1074.html)

- [ ] **O3 · Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD; "Indian Niño")** — SST seesaw across the equatorial Indian Ocean; western pole 50–70°E, 10°S–10°N; eastern pole 90–110°E, 10°S–0. Monitored by the Dipole Mode Index (DMI = west − east SST anomaly): positive DMI ≥ +0.4 °C, negative ≤ −0.4 °C, sustained several weeks. Develops in boreal summer, peaks Sep–Nov. Positive IOD enhances moisture transport → favours above-normal ISMR and **can offset El Niño** (1997 stayed normal; strongest recent positive events 1997 and 2019, DMI up to +1.78); negative suppresses rain. NE monsoon positively correlated with ENSO/IOD. Cyclones: BoB TC frequency highest in La Niña/negative-IOD years, but the **most severe** cyclones occur in La Niña/positive-IOD years; positive IOD shifts genesis toward the southern BoB (Mahala et al. 2015).
  - Sources: [mausam.imd.gov.in · IOD index](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/responsive/iod_index.php) · [IMD · ENSO–IOD Update Bulletin](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/ClimateInformation/imdweb/CLIMATE_FCST/Bulletin/ENSO_IOD_Update_Bulletin.pdf) · [IMD · NE Monsoon monograph](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/contents/met_monograph.pdf) · [Mahala et al. study](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/nathaz/v75y2015i2p1105-1125.html)

- [ ] **O4 · Madden–Julian Oscillation (MJO)** — eastward-moving, planetary-scale pulse of deep convection over the Indian and Pacific Oceans; 30–60-day cycle divided into 8 phases (~7–8 days each); enhances convection over the Indian Ocean while suppressing it over the Pacific. Drives active/break spells of the ISM beyond ~1 week; strongly modulates genesis/intensity of cyclonic disturbances over the North Indian Ocean (monsoon genesis favoured in phases 4–5; post-monsoon phases 3–4).
  - Sources: [IMD · Monsoon FAQ](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/monsoonfaq.pdf) · [MAUSAM journal](https://mausamjournal.imd.gov.in/index.php/MAUSAM/article/view/316) · [MAUSAM journal (PDF)](https://mausamjournal.imd.gov.in/index.php/MAUSAM/article/download/1251/1082/4609)

- [ ] **O5 · Pacific Decadal Oscillation (PDO)** — slow Pacific basin SST pattern; IMD's Monsoon FAQ lists PDO among the contributing factors governing year-to-year southwest monsoon variation (alongside NAO). Interdecadal Pacific Oscillation (IPO): no direct IMD material found — marginal/unclear.
  - Source: [IMD · Monsoon FAQ](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/monsoonfaq.pdf)

- [ ] **O6 · Indian Ocean Basin Mode (IOBM) / Indian Ocean warming** — dominant interannual SST mode of the tropical Indian Ocean; warm IOB peaks in spring, and in summer its extra moisture is transported to South Asia, increasing monsoon precipitation. Trend: tropical Indian Ocean SST rose ~1 °C (1951–2015); IITM work shows rapid warming weakens the land–sea thermal gradient, dampening the monsoon Hadley circulation and drying central-east/northern India.
  - Sources: [J. Climate · IOB–IOD study (PDF)](https://aos.wisc.edu/~zliu/publications/10_JCLI_IOBIOD.pdf) · [Nature Communications (PDF)](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms8423.pdf) · [PIB · MoES statement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2242357)

- [ ] **O7 · North Atlantic Oscillation (NAO) & Atlantic Multidecadal Oscillation (AMO)** — NAO is listed by IMD among factors influencing the ISM, but no strong deterministic link appears in the fetched material. AMO: multidecadal North Atlantic SST mode; positive AMO phases coincide with wetter Indian monsoon decades (e.g., 1926–65) and paleo-records show a coherent AMO–ISM response, especially over central India.
  - Sources: [IMD · ENSO & IOD FAQ](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/FAQ_ENSO_IOD.pdf) · [Nature Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-58265-3)

## 3 · Cyclone & storm systems (S)

- [ ] **S1 · Tropical cyclones over the North Indian Ocean** (IMD: cyclonic disturbances — depression and above) — Bay of Bengal + Arabian Sea, monitored round-the-clock by RSMC New Delhi. **Season:** two peaks — IMD's "storm season" Apr–May and Oct–Dec; monthly frequency is bi-modal with primary peak in **November**, secondary in **May**; May–Jun/Oct–Nov produce the severe-intensity systems. **Regions:** east coast (Odisha, West Bengal, Andhra, Tamil Nadu) and Gujarat/Konkan coasts. ~5 cyclones/year ≈ **5–6% of the global annual average (~80)**, with a **4:1 Bay:Arabian ratio**; 13 disturbances formed in 2024 vs an 11.2/year normal (1965–2023); the basin is disproportionately deadly (1970 Bangladesh cyclone: ≥300,000 deaths from storm tide).
  - Sources: [RSMC New Delhi · terminology (PDF)](https://rsmcnewdelhi.imd.gov.in/images/pdf/terminology.pdf) · [RSMC · FAQ (PDF)](https://rsmcnewdelhi.imd.gov.in/images/pdf/faq.pdf) · [RSMC · 2024 report](https://rsmcnewdelhi.imd.gov.in/download.php?path=uploads/report/27/27_a7fb96_27_ef4e32_RSMC-Report2024-for%20upload.pdf)

- [ ] **S2 · IMD intensity classification** — Depression (31–49 km/h) → Deep Depression (50–61) → Cyclonic Storm (62–88) → Severe Cyclonic Storm (89–117; ≥48 kt) → Very Severe (118–166) → Extremely Severe (167–221; 90–119 kt) → **Super Cyclonic Storm (≥222 km/h / ≥120 kt)**. WMO-adopted standard driving warning levels and damage expectations.
  - Sources: [RSMC · hazard (PDF)](https://rsmcnewdelhi.imd.gov.in/download.php?path=uploads/climatology/hazard.pdf) · [RSMC · damage (PDF)](https://rsmcnewdelhi.imd.gov.in/images/pdf/damage.pdf) · [RSMC · terminology (PDF)](https://rsmcnewdelhi.imd.gov.in/images/pdf/terminology.pdf)

- [ ] **S3 · Storm surge / storm tide** — abnormal sea-level rise at cyclone landfall (wind-driven), added on top of the astronomical tide. **Season:** with landfalling cyclones of both peak seasons. **Regions:** low-lying deltas — Odisha, West Bengal, Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat. "Storm surges are by far the greatest killers in a cyclone": coastal inundation, beach/embankment erosion, loss of soil fertility, drinking-water pollution and epidemics; IMD issues dedicated Storm Surge Warnings.
  - Sources: [RSMC · FAQ (PDF)](https://rsmcnewdelhi.imd.gov.in/images/pdf/faq.pdf) · [mausam.imd.gov.in · cyclone information](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/responsive/cycloneinformation.php)

- [ ] **S4 · Western Disturbance** — synoptic-scale extra-tropical system in the middle-latitude westerlies: a cyclonic circulation/trough (mid–lower troposphere) or surface low originating over the Mediterranean, Caspian and Black Seas, moving across Iraq–Iran–Afghanistan–Pakistan into NW India. **Season:** winter (Dec–Feb), ~6–7 disturbances/month incl. induced lows. **Regions:** Western Himalaya (rain/snow), NW plains; the rainfall belt may extend east to West Bengal/Assam. **Impact:** principal winter precipitation source; active Dec–Jan WD precipitation is beneficial for rabi crops (wheat).
  - Sources: [IMD Pune · glossary](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/Reports/glossary.pdf) · [IMD Pune · Synoptic Meteorology training notes](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/training/training%20notes/Synoptic%20Meteorology-IMTC.pdf) · [IMD Pune · forecasting manual](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/Reports/Forecasting_Mannuals/IND_III-1.1.pdf) · [PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=977)

- [ ] **S5 · Nor'wester / Kalbaisakhi** — severe pre-monsoon convective thunderstorms and squalls of eastern/NE India (IMD field studies since 1931–41; STORM programme 2007–2010). **Season:** Mar–May, max May. **Regions:** West Bengal (Kalbaisakhi), Assam–NE India ("Bardoli Chheerha" is the local name — not found on fetched IMD pages); cells initiate over Jharkhand/Odisha/Bangladesh and organize into mesoscale convective complexes. **Impact:** violent squalls, hail, heavy rain; high-frequency thunderstorm zone, key for nowcasting.
  - Sources: [IMD Pune · Synoptic Meteorology training notes](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/training/training%20notes/Synoptic%20Meteorology-IMTC.pdf) · [MAUSAM journal](https://mausamjournal.imd.gov.in/index.php/MAUSAM/article/view/1446)

- [ ] **S6 · Dust storms / Andhi** — convective sand/dust storms of NW India; with little moisture, downdrafts from cumulonimbus raise dust. **Season:** Apr–Jun (pre-monsoon). **Regions:** Rajasthan and adjoining NW India. **Impact:** visibility below 1 km, dust transport and strong squalls; triggered by intense surface heating plus WDs/cyclonic circulations.
  - Source: [IMD Pune · Synoptic Meteorology training notes](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/training/training%20notes/Synoptic%20Meteorology-IMTC.pdf)

- [ ] **S7 · Thunderstorms, hailstorms & squall lines** — pre-monsoon (Mar–May) is India's peak convective season ("clear skies, intense heating, large instability"); violent thunderstorms, squalls, heavy precipitation and hail common over NE India; squall lines are mesoscale lines of storms ("pre-cyclone squall lines" appear in cyclone radar structure). **Impact:** lightning/hail crop and property damage; parent phenomena of Andhi and Nor'wester.
  - Sources: [IMD Pune · Synoptic Meteorology training notes](https://www.imdpune.gov.in/training/training%20notes/Synoptic%20Meteorology-IMTC.pdf) · [RSMC · FAQ (PDF)](https://rsmcnewdelhi.imd.gov.in/images/pdf/faq.pdf)

- [ ] **S8 · Tornadoes** — rare over India; IMD records yielded **44 tornado cases** (plus 113 strong-wind reports from 1951) used to map distribution; preferred zones are mesoscale lows along lower-tropospheric lines of discontinuity, forming at the tail of violent thunderstorms/squall lines. **Impact:** narrow-strip destructive winds; rare but consequential.
  - Source: [MAUSAM journal](https://mausamjournal.imd.gov.in/index.php/MAUSAM/article/view/3391)

## 4 · Extremes & local phenomena (X)

- [ ] **X1 · Heat wave / severe heat wave (warm night)** — declared when a station max ≥40 °C (plains), ≥37 °C (coast), ≥30 °C (hills); departure 4.5–6.4 °C = heat wave, >6.4 °C = severe; outright ≥45/≥47 °C; needs ≥2 stations in a subdivision for 2 consecutive days. **Season:** Mar–Jun (peak May). **Regions:** NW & central India ("heat wave zone") and east coast (Andhra Pradesh, Odisha). **Impact:** heat stress/mortality (May 2015 SE-India event >2,500 deaths), crop/water stress; increasing frequency — 2025 was the 8th-warmest year (+0.28 °C vs 1991–2020), 2024 the warmest (+0.65 °C).
  - Sources: [IMD · heat/cold wave definitions (PDF)](https://internal.imd.gov.in/section/nhac/dynamic/Definition.pdf) · [IMD Pune · cold & heat wave monograph (PDF)](https://imdpune.gov.in/Reports/Met_Monograph_Cold_Heat_Waves.pdf) · [WMO · statement on the climate of India 2025](https://wmo.int/media/news-from-members/statement-climate-of-india-during-2025)

- [ ] **X2 · Loo** — hot, dry, oppressive pre-monsoon wind, a "marked feature of summers in northern India" (IMD); blows from afternoon into midnight; evening dust storms common in May. **Season:** Apr–Jun. **Regions:** Punjab, Haryana, E. Rajasthan, UP. **Impact:** intense surface heating, heat-stress risk on direct exposure, feeds dust storms.
  - Sources: [IMD · forecasting SOP (PDF)](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/contents/pdf/forecasting_sop.pdf) · [NCERT · India Physical Environment](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/kegy104.pdf)

- [ ] **X3 · Cold wave / severe cold wave (cold day)** — station minimum ≤10 °C (plains), ≤0 °C (hills); departure −4.5 to −6.4 °C = cold wave, ≤−6.4 °C = severe; outright ≤4 °C / ≤2 °C; coastal criterion if −4.5 °C departure and min ≤15 °C. **Season:** Nov–Mar, worst Dec–Jan. **Regions:** NW & central India (Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, UP, Rajasthan, MP, Bihar, WB, Jharkhand, Odisha…). **Impact:** ~900 deaths in Jan 2003; rabi crop losses; NW winds from Central Asia/Hindukush; frequency decreasing as winter minimums warm.
  - Sources: [IMD · heat/cold wave FAQ (PDF)](https://internal.imd.gov.in/section/nhac/dynamic/faq.pdf) · [IMD Pune · hazard atlas, cold (PDF)](https://imdpune.gov.in/hazardatlas/cold.pdf)

- [ ] **X4 · Winter fog** — large-scale episodic IGP fog spells lasting weeks in peak winter; radiation fog after western disturbances. **Season:** Dec–Jan (Nov & Feb occasionally). **Regions:** Indo-Gangetic plains — the most fog-vulnerable region globally per IMD; densest spells drop visibility below 50 m. **Impact:** aviation/rail/road disruption (IMD fog warnings for 12 airports incl. Delhi, Lucknow, Jaipur, Amritsar); ~120–159 road deaths in the extreme Dec–Jan seasons of 2016-17/2017-18.
  - Source: [IMD · Fog Information and Warning Services (PDF)](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/contents/pdf/pubbrochures/Fog%20Information%20and%20Warning%20Services.pdf)

- [ ] **X5 · Frost / ground frost** — frost "prevails when temperature falls below 0 °C in an area where it is abnormal"; NDMA (citing IMD) frost/freeze warning criterion: winds <16 km/h and air temp <0 °C on sub-zero winter nights. **Season:** Dec–Feb. **Regions:** cold-wave-prone NW/central plains and Himalayan foothills. **Impact:** rabi crop and livestock damage; ≥33% crop loss qualifies for national relief.
  - Source: [NDMA · cold wave & frost guidelines (PDF)](https://ndma.gov.in/sites/default/files/PDF/Guidelines/Guidelines-on-Cold-Wave-and-Frost.pdf)

- [ ] **X6 · Mango showers** — end-of-summer pre-monsoon showers ("blossom"/cherry-blossom or coffee showers, not an IMD criteria term) that hasten mango ripening in Kerala and coastal Karnataka; blossom showers make coffee flower. **Season:** Mar–May (Apr–May peak). **Regions:** Kerala, coastal Karnataka, Western Ghats. **Impact:** beneficial — moistens land and supports mango/coffee/spice crops before the monsoon.
  - Source: [NCERT · India Physical Environment](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/kegy104.pdf)

- [ ] **X7 · October heat** — retreating southwest monsoon brings clear skies and rising temperatures over still-moist land; combined heat and humidity make weather "oppressive" — the "October heat"; the mercury then falls rapidly over north India. **Season:** Oct–Nov (post-monsoon). **Regions:** peninsular India / northern-plains transition zone. **Impact:** pre-winter heat stress before the northeast-monsoon rains.
  - Source: [NCERT · India Physical Environment](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/kegy104.pdf)

- [ ] **X8 · Urban heat island (UHI) effect** — asphalt/concrete cities run hotter than their surroundings; NDMA's 2019 heat-wave guidelines require cities to assess UHI factors in Heat Action Plans (23 states, 195 districts, 64 cities). **Worst:** summer nights. **Regions:** Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Bengaluru, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Jaipur, etc. **Impact:** amplifies heat-wave night temperatures; Delhi SAPCC 2.0 flags heat as top vulnerability (IMD 1991–2020 data); summer max projected +1.5 °C (SSP2-4.5) to +2.1 °C (SSP5-8.5) by the 2050s.
  - Source: [PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2286772&reg=48&lang=1)

- [ ] **X9 · Atmospheric brown cloud (ABC)** — haze of sulphates, nitrates, organics, black carbon, dust and fly ash; dominant over India in winter, low in the summer monsoon; highlighted by INDOEX 1996–99. **Regions:** IGP, Arabian Sea, Bay of Bengal, North Indian Ocean. **Impact:** solar dimming; MoES assessment links post-1950s summer-monsoon rainfall decline to anthropogenic aerosols, while IGP absorbing aerosols can invigorate pre-monsoon rain.
  - Sources: [PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/erelcontent.aspx?relid=36456) · [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-15-4327-2_5)

## 5 · Long-term change drivers (L)

- [ ] **L1 · Global warming signals for India (MoES/IMD)** — surface air temperature over India rose ~0.7 °C (1901–2018); tropical Indian Ocean SST +~1 °C (1951–2015). Increased monsoon variability and extremes: rising localized heavy rainfall and droughts, higher tropical-cyclone intensity; central/northern India and the Western Himalaya show more extreme precipitation; elevation-dependent Himalayan warming. IMD products: Climate Hazard & Vulnerability Atlas; 29 "Observed Rainfall Variability" state reports.
  - Source: [PIB · MoES assessment](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2242357)

- [ ] **L2 · Himalayan cryosphere & GLOF risk** — HKH glaciers retreating 14.9±15.1 m/yr (Ganga 15.5±14.4, Brahmaputra 20.2±19.7); Chandra basin lost ~6% area in 20 yrs; elevation-dependent warming, all seasons. **Regions:** Hindu Kush–Himalaya; glaciated Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra basins. **Impact:** new/enlarging glacial lakes raise GLOF risk — NGRMP phase-I (HP, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal, J&K, Ladakh) builds early warning; South Lhonak and Shako Cho (Sikkim) monitored in real time.
  - Sources: [PIB archive release](https://archive.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=279800) · [PIB · MoES assessment](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2242357&reg=1&lang=1)
